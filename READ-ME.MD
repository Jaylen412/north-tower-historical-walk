NORTH TOWER — AN EVERYDAY WORLD
Interactive historical interpretation, circa 2000–early 2001

RUNNING THE TOUR
1. Extract this entire archive.
2. With Python 3 installed, run: python3 run-tour.py
   On Windows, use: py run-tour.py
3. Your browser opens automatically. Keep the server window open while exploring.
Do not open world/index.html directly: browser modules require a local web server.
The tour runs on your computer. Internet access is only used for fonts and historical source links.

PS5 CONTROLLER
Pair the controller in your device's Bluetooth settings. Open the tour and press ×.
Left stick: move. Click L3 to toggle running at twice walking speed; click again to walk.
Right stick: look. ×: enter / interact / select.
Circle: close panel. Triangle: checkpoint menu. Options: guide and settings.
Left stick or D-pad: move the highlighted menu selection. Right stick: scroll panel text.
Hold a direction to repeat. L1 / R1: previous / next checkpoint during walking or reading.
D-pad or left stick left/right adjusts focused sliders and graphics choices.
Triangle opens the checkpoint list from any panel. Circle closes panels.
The browser must expose a standard controller mapping. A wired connection is an alternative.

KEYBOARD AND TOUCH
WASD / arrow keys: walk. Drag mouse: look. Click the world: capture mouse.
E: interact. G: checkpoints. Escape: release mouse / pause.
On touch devices, use movement arrows and drag the view.
Use Performance graphics in Guide & controls if the scene runs slowly.

SCOPE AND ACCURACY
Three walkable spaces: lobby, representative office, and interpretive dining room.
An abbreviated elevator cabin journey connects them. Eight sourced checkpoints include discussion prompts.
This is a functional 3D prototype, not a hyper-realistic or surveyed architectural replica.
It is not a model of every floor. Circulation, proportions, furnishings, elevator routing and travel time,
planting and decorative details, and the distant city are approximate. The office is not a particular tenant's suite.
The restaurant is not a measured reproduction of its 1996 refurbishment. There is no attack simulation.
The public observation deck was in the South Tower; Windows on the World was in the North Tower.
Visited checkpoints and preferences are saved only in this browser's local storage.
